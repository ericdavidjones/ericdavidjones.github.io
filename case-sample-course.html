Sample course design brief

An original onboarding course for a fictional repair-shop app. It shows how I design software training end to end: from performance goal to practice to assessment.

The problem

Gearloft is a fictional cloud app for small bike and small-engine repair shops. Its new customers often come from paper tickets, and the habits that come with paper, like missed parts and unbilled labor, carry over into the software. If a new shop doesn't invoice a real job in its first week, it's at risk of giving up on the product.

Performance goal: within their first week, a new user creates, completes and invoices a real work order without contacting support.

The learner

Sam, an owner-mechanic at a two-person shop, is comfortable with a phone but not with business software. Sam learns in short bursts between jobs, on the laptop at the front counter, and cares about one thing: getting paid for the work.

Design decisions

  1. One story, start to finish. Every lesson follows the same customer and the same mower, from walk-in to invoice. The learner sees the whole workflow as one job, not as disconnected features.
  2. Teach the mistakes that cost money. Duplicate customers, vague problem descriptions, unbilled diagnosis time and work started before approval each get their own activity, because that's where shops lose money.
  3. Practice in a safe copy of the product. A 10-step simulation lets learners run the whole job, with guided and try-it modes and specific feedback on every wrong click.
  4. Assess with scenarios, not recall. Every knowledge-check question puts the learner at the shop counter facing a decision, and each one maps to an objective.
  5. Leave something behind. A one-page printable job aid supports the learner on the job, after the course is over.

Objectives and how each is practiced

How it was built

I wrote the storyboard, objectives, scenarios, assessment and all learner-facing text. I don't hold a personal Articulate 360 license, so instead of Rise I built the course as a responsive web course, with an AI coding assistant to help produce the code. It uses Rise-style patterns (process steps, labeled graphics, flip cards, tabs, accordions) and is keyboard-accessible and works on a phone.

What I'd measure in a real rollout

  • The share of new shops that send their first invoice within 7 days
  • Support contacts about work orders and invoicing in a shop's first 30 days
  • Knowledge-check items most often missed, to find the next course revision
