Case study · Fullbay

One course, two audiences

Fullbay rebuilt its core platform from the ground up. I designed the first courses for it, each in two versions: one for customers and one for the internal teams who support them.

Role
Instructional designer
Timeline
2–3 weeks per course
Scope
2 courses × 2 versions
Partners
Product SMEs + 8 internal orgs
Result
First 2 of a 19-course plan

The challenge

Fullbay was releasing a reimagined, re-engineered version of its core platform. It wasn't an incremental update; it was a new way to run the same business. Two very different audiences needed to learn it at the same time:

  • Customers, who needed to do their daily work in the new product.
  • Internal teams (Support, Sales, Onboarding and Customer Success), who needed all of that plus the internal knowledge to sell, set up and support it.

My role

I scoped, designed and built the first two courses in the new product's curriculum, Foundations and Parts, each in a customer version and an internal version. I worked with product SMEs on the content and gathered feedback from leaders across eight internal organizations. Seventeen more courses were planned in the pipeline.

The approach

  1. Build the shared core once. Both versions teach the same workflows, so the procedural content stayed identical, and accurate, across audiences.
  2. Let the experts draw the line. What's internal-only isn't always obvious. I decided it through conversations with SMEs and representatives from each department, not by guessing.
  3. Rewrite the voice for customers. Customer versions speak directly to the person doing the work: "your shop," "your inventory," "when you perform this action." Internal terminology was audited out.
  4. Review at a roundtable. Leaders from eight internal organizations reviewed the final drafts together, so conflicting feedback got resolved in the room rather than across email threads.

The result

Internal learners, SMEs and stakeholders gave the courses very positive feedback during review, and I was recognized for how I partnered with product and department leaders through the build and feedback process. They were the first two courses of a planned 19-course curriculum for the new platform.

What I'd do next

With 17 courses still to build, I'd formalize the pattern: a single-source structure where shared lessons live in one place and the internal-only material sits in clearly separated add-on lessons. A product change would then mean one update, not two, and keeping both audiences accurate would scale with the curriculum.
