Lesson 1: Start the Work Order

Lesson 1 of 4

Start the Work Order

A good start makes the rest of the job easy.

A work order is the home for everything about one job: who the customer is, what they brought in, what's wrong, and what you did about it.

When the start is right, the estimate, the approval, and the invoice all build on it. When it's wrong, like a duplicate customer or a vague problem description, the mistake follows the job all the way to the bill.

Four Steps to Open a Work Order

Step through each one. You'll practice them yourself at the end of the course.

Step 1 of 4
Step 1

Click New Work Order

From the Gearloft dashboard, click New Work Order in the top-right corner. Every job starts here, even a quick tune-up.

Step 2

Find the Customer

Search by last name or phone number. If the customer has been in before, select them. Only click Add customer when the search comes up empty.

Step 3

Choose the Equipment

Pick the item they brought in today from their equipment list. If it's new to you, add it with its type, make, and model so it's on file next time.

Step 4

Describe the Problem

Write what's wrong, when it happens, and what you've already checked. Then click Save. The work order is open and ready for labor and parts.

Tip

Search before you add. Two records for the same customer split their history. Next time, you won't see what you fixed last spring, or what they paid.

Lesson 2: Add Labor and Parts

Lesson 2 of 4

Add Labor and Parts

If it isn't on the work order, it doesn't get paid.

Everything you do and everything you use on a job belongs on the work order. That's what turns it into an accurate estimate now and an accurate invoice later.

Gearloft splits every job into two kinds of lines: labor (your time) and parts (what you install). Select each tab to compare them.

Labor Is Your Time

  • Add a labor line for each task: diagnosis, repair, and adjustments.
  • Enter the time in hours. 15 minutes is 0.25. Gearloft multiplies it by your shop's labor rate.
  • Short tasks count. Twenty minutes of adjusting is twenty minutes of skilled work.

Parts Are What You Install

  • Add parts from your inventory so the price fills in and your stock count drops automatically.
  • Add every part as you use it, not from memory at the end of the day.
  • Not in inventory? Add it as a one-time part with its cost and price.

Watch for this

Small tasks are the ones that go missing. Diagnosis, draining fuel, a quick adjustment: each takes only minutes, but across a month they add up to hours of unpaid work. Add a line the moment you finish a task.

Lesson 3: Get Approval

Lesson 3 of 4

Get Approval

No surprises at pickup.

Before you start work, the customer approves the estimate. It takes a minute, and it prevents the most uncomfortable conversation in any shop: "I never agreed to that."

In Gearloft, the work order status moves from Open to Approved when the customer says yes. That's your green light.

Three Ways to Get a Yes

Choose the one that fits how the customer is reaching you. Select each option to learn how it works.

By Text Link Most Common
Click Send estimate by text. The customer gets a link, reviews the lines, and taps Approve. Gearloft changes the status to Approved and records the time on its own.
In Person at the Counter
Turn the screen toward the customer, walk through the estimate, and have them tap Approve. That works best when they're waiting while you diagnose.
Over the Phone
Read the total and the main lines out loud. When they agree, click Record approval and note who approved, the amount, and the time. Never skip the note; it's your only record.

What Would You Do?

Maria approved the $114.50 estimate by text this morning. Halfway through the job, Sam finds a cracked carburetor gasket. Replacing it adds a $12 part and 0.5 hours of labor, so the new total is $166.50.

What should Sam do?

Important

Don't start work until the status says Approved. If the price changes mid-job, update the estimate and get approval again before you continue.

Lesson 4: Complete and Invoice

Lesson 4 of 4

Complete and Invoice

Finish the job and get paid for all of it.

You don't have to build the invoice. Gearloft makes it from the work order. Your job is to make sure the work order is complete and correct before it goes out.

From Finished Job to Sent Invoice

Step 1 of 4
Step 1

Check Every Line

Compare the work order to what you actually did. Is every task a labor line, and is every part you used a parts line?

Step 2

Mark the Job Complete

Click Mark complete. The status changes to Complete, and Gearloft builds the invoice from the work order.

Step 3

Review the Invoice

Check the customer's contact details and the total, and add a short note if it helps, like care tips for the customer.

Step 4

Send It

Click Send invoice. The customer gets a text or email with a link to pay. After it's sent, the invoice is locked, so check before you click.

Before You Click Send

  • Every task is a labor line, including diagnosis and small adjustments.
  • Every part you used is a parts line.
  • The total matches what the customer approved.
  • The customer's phone number or email is correct.

Check it, complete it, then send it. A sent invoice is locked.
