A six-course onboarding program

Live onboarding didn't scale for the smallest customers. I designed Single User Shop Essentials, a self-guided program that takes a one-person shop through setup with no calls required.

Role
Instructional designer
Timeline
10 weeks
Scope
6 courses + capstone
Partners
Onboarding, Customer Success
Result
Live with new customers

The challenge

Single-user shops, often one owner-mechanic, needed the same onboarding as larger customers, but the time the onboarding team spent on calls and online sessions with them didn't match the return. The business needed a way to onboard these shops well without a person on the line.

The ask: a self-guided, online-only onboarding program built for single-user shops.

My role

This was my first major project at Fullbay. I owned the program design and build: the research, the program agenda, all six courses, the knowledge checks and the capstone assessment. I partnered with the Onboarding and Customer Success teams throughout.

The approach

  1. Start from what onboarding specialists actually do. I studied the standard employee-led onboarding process and reviewed real onboarding interactions logged in Salesforce. The program agenda mirrors the order a specialist would walk a customer through.
  2. Six short courses, not one long one. A shop owner learns between jobs. Short, focused courses let them make progress in the time they have and come back to the part they need.
  3. Check the key idea at the end of every module. Each module ends with one knowledge-check question on its main takeaway, giving quick reinforcement without slowing anyone down.
  4. Finish with proof of readiness. A 10–12 question capstone covers the whole program, so both the customer and Fullbay know the shop is ready to run on its own.
  5. Work alongside in-app help. The product team's in-app guides (built in Pendo) supported learners inside the software; the eLearning gave them the context and the why.

The result

The program launched and began onboarding new single-user customers without live sessions. Roughly half a dozen new customers were onboarded through it, and course ratings stayed high. It gave the Onboarding team a repeatable, self-guided path for its smallest accounts.

What I'd do differently

I'd spend more time on discovery at the start. As my first major project, I learned how much a few extra questions up front save later. I'd also agree on success measures with Onboarding and Customer Success at kickoff, such as time to a shop's first invoice and support contacts in the first 30 days. That way the program's impact shows up in numbers the business already tracks.
